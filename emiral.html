Inside the bottle

Three plants carry the blend.

The elixirs are built on a concentrated herbal blend. These are the three ingredients the manufacturer names on the pack.

01

Licorice Root

Glycyrrhiza glabra · special variety from Asia and Egypt

The base of the blend, and one of the most widely used plants in herbal practice anywhere — its record of traditional use runs from ancient Egypt through to West African preparations today. It is also what gives the elixirs their distinctive sweet-bitter finish.

02

Carica Papaya

Papaya leaf

A familiar ingredient in West African home herbal preparations and a natural source of plant enzymes. Most Nigerian households will already know the leaf, which is part of why the blend is an easy one to introduce.

03

Brassica Oleracea

The cabbage and kale family

A dense source of everyday plant nutrients and the third ingredient named on the manufacturer’s pack. Between the three, the blend is entirely plant-derived.

Using the product

Practical, not medical.

How to store and prepare what you order. For how much to take, the pack is the only instruction that counts.

  • The elixirs

    Shake the bottle well before pouring — the blend settles. Take with water; the taste is strongly herbal and does not suit everyone straight.

  • The teas

    Brew like ordinary tea. One sachet in hot water, left to steep for a few minutes, then drink plain or with honey.

  • Dosage

    Follow the directions printed on your pack. This page does not set a dose, and you should not take more than the pack states.

  • Storage

    Keep cool and out of direct sunlight, cap closed. Store out of reach of children.

  • Before you start

    If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, taking prescribed medicine, or managing a health condition, speak with your doctor or pharmacist before starting any herbal supplement.

  • Not a replacement

    A herbal supplement is not a substitute for prescribed treatment. Never stop medicine your doctor has given you in order to take one.
